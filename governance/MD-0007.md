## Decision

1. **Upstream de-identification and downstream format conversion are distinct acts.** They are performed by different parties at different times for different purposes, and VIDS records them in different fields.

2. **`DeIdentification.Tool` names the tool of the de-identification act only.** The field denotes the act of de-identifying. It may name only the tool that performed that act.

3. **Incidental metadata loss resulting from format conversion does not, by itself, constitute a documented de-identification act or make the conversion utility the de-identification tool.** A converter that drops header fields as a side effect of writing a different container may reduce identifiability, but it has not performed the act §8.2 records. Naming it in `DeIdentification.Tool` misstates what was done to the data.

4. **Source-provided de-identification provenance stays attributed to the source.** Where the de-identification was performed upstream, the recorded tool, method and date are the source's, recorded faithfully at whatever granularity the source provides.

5. **A local conversion date must not be recorded as an upstream de-identification date.** Where the de-identification date is not available from the source, the field carries an approved recording-state sentinel, not a substitute value.

## Rationale

MD-0006 establishes the general assertion-discipline rule. This decision applies that rule to the semantics of `DeIdentification.Tool` in SPEC §8.2.

§8.2 is an escalate-only section, so this decision memo precedes any edit to it.

The defect is in the specification, not in adopter practice. SPEC.md:473 gives the `DeIdentification.Tool` candidate list as `"dcm2niix | CTP | pydicom | manual"`, placing a format converter beside CTP, which is a de-identification tool. EXAMPLES.md:115 and QUICKSTART.md:154 carry the same pairing as copyable worked examples, joining `Method: HIPAA Safe Harbor` to `Tool: dcm2niix`. An adopter who reads the specification, copies the example and populates the field as shown produces a record asserting that a format converter applied Safe Harbor. The pipeline author who did exactly this followed the specification correctly.

The distinction matters because `DeIdentification` is the field a downstream reader consults to answer what was done to the data and by whom. A record naming the wrong act does not merely lose precision; it documents an act that did not occur. That is the failure mode the standard exists to prevent, and it is the same root cause addressed at the generator level in MD-0006: a field populated on the strength of an act other than the one it denotes.

The alternative of leaving the candidate list intact and correcting only the worked examples was set aside. The list is where the adopter looks first, and correcting the derivative without the source leaves the defect reproducible.

## Application

This decision governs the following corrections, which ship as one erratum release alongside MD-0006:

| Artifact | Location | Correction |
|----------|----------|------------|
| SPEC.md | line 473, §8.2 | Remove `dcm2niix` from the `DeIdentification.Tool` candidate list. State that the field names the tool of the de-identification act only. |
| EXAMPLES.md | line 115 | Correct `Tool: dcm2niix` under `DeIdentification`. |
| QUICKSTART.md | line 154 | Same correction. |

Change taxonomy: defect-fix. Per the registered taxonomy, the counterfactual test applies: the specification listed a format converter as a de-identification tool, which was false before this decision existed and would remain false without it. This decision records the semantics and authorizes the correction; it does not create the error, so `governance-directed` does not apply.

**Do not touch.** `ConversionTool: dcm2niix` at SPEC.md:450, QUICKSTART.md:143, EXAMPLES.md:95, `tools/vids_init.py`:221 and six instances in `tests/generate_test_fixtures.py`. These are conversion records and are correct as written. Narrowing `DeIdentification.Tool` is what makes `ConversionTool` the right field for a converter, so a blanket search-and-remove of `dcm2niix` would break the corrected model.

The generator needs no change under this decision: `tools/vids_init.py` already writes `Tool: "TODO"`. Its `DeIdentification.Date: today` defect is governed by MD-0006 clause 2 and is corrected there.

**Conformance impact is nil.** Verified 2026-07-27 against the shipped artifact `vids_validator/validator.py` from `vids_validator-1.2.1-py3-none-any.whl` (PyPI, MD5 `32462465a70eaf8c0025e40b8ec6c400`, reporting `ValidatorVersion: 1.2.1`) and against `VALIDATION_RULES.md` on repository `main`. Neither contains any reference to `DeIdentification` or any of its subfields. The string at SPEC.md:473 is a pipe-separated placeholder inside an illustrative example block, not a schema enumeration, and §8.2 requires only `VIDSVersion` within the sidecar, with all other fields RECOMMENDED. No dataset's validation outcome changes. The correction is an erratum, not a rule change, and the pull request must record that finding.

## Scope

This decision fixes the meaning of one field. It does not:

- Change any dataset-conformance requirement or validation rule. Only SPEC does that, and only through the ratification path in CONTRIBUTING.md.
- Change the requirement level of any `DeIdentification` subfield. They remain RECOMMENDED.
- Touch `ConversionTool` or the conversion-record model.
- Enumerate an approved list of de-identification tools. Removing an incorrect entry is not an endorsement of the remainder.
- Determine which de-identification method Princeton Medical Systems applies or documents in any commercial engagement. That is a separate operational decision outside the standard.
- Cover assertion vocabulary or generator provenance population. That is MD-0006.
